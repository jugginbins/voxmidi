Your voice, as a MIDI instrument.

VoxMidi turns singing, humming and beatboxing into live notes, pitch bend, chords and drum hits. It is free, open source, and made for FL Studio on Windows and Mac.

Everything your voice can control

Each part of the plugin works like a track in your session. Use one, or all of them at once.

  • Melody

    Notes and velocity

    Your pitch becomes MIDI notes roughly 30 ms after you start singing. A note starts only once your pitch has settled, so a scoop into the note or a wobble does not create extra notes.

    Try the stickiness, pitch confidence and gate settings. Louder singing sends higher velocity.

  • Scales and key

    Stay in tune

    Fifteen scales plus a custom one, on any root note. Notes snap to the scale, which makes everything more forgiving. Sing a few notes and VoxMidi suggests the key you are in.

  • Pitch bend

    Slides and vibrato

    IntelliBend holds the note until you really bend away from it. TruBend follows every move your voice makes. A moving pitch becomes a bend, and a pitch that stays somewhere new becomes a new note.

    Set the bend range to match your synth, from 1 to 24 semitones.

  • Chords

    Own MIDI channel

    Sing one note and get a chord: triads and sevenths that follow your scale, power chords, octaves, sus2, sus4, major and minor. Chords go out on a separate channel, so melody and chords can drive different instruments.

  • Beatbox

    Eight drum pads

    Train each pad with your own sounds: a kick, a snare, a hat, a click or a clap. Hit it six to ten times and VoxMidi learns what it sounds like. In tests, hits fired about 7 ms after the sound.

    Sounds that match no pad are ignored, so a sung note does not set off a drum.

  • Vowels and loudness

    Controller data

    The vowels "ah", "ee" and "oo" become controller values on CC 20, 21 and 22, and loudness goes to CC 23. Map them to filter cutoff, vibrato depth or anything else your synth can learn. You can re-learn the vowels for your own voice.

There are two plugins. VoxMidi is an instrument that listens to your microphone itself, and is the one to use in FL Studio. VoxMidi FX is an effect that takes audio from a mixer insert, for hosts that pass MIDI out of effects. Looking for something else? See all releases, including Linux.

Install on Windows

Unzip, then copy both .vst3 folders to:

C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3

Install on Mac

Unzip, copy the plugins to /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, then clear the download quarantine in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine \
  "/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/VoxMidi.vst3"

Set it up in FL Studio

  1. Scan for the pluginOpen Options, then Manage plugins, then Find plugins. Make sure your VST3 folder is in the list.
  2. Add VoxMidi to the Channel rackSet Audio source to "Direct", then press "Audio input..." and pick your microphone. Sing, and the note display and level bar should react.
  3. Send its MIDI outOpen the plugin's wrapper settings (the gear icon), go to the Settings tab, and under MIDI set the Output port to 1.
  4. Add the synth you want to playIn that synth's wrapper settings, set the Input port to 1 as well.
  5. Match the bend rangeSet the synth's pitch bend range to the same number as VoxMidi's Bend range (2 by default). Now sing.

FL Studio's manual describes matching port numbers for plugin MIDI, but does not list exact steps for plugin-to-plugin routing. If a menu looks different in your version, the idea is the same: use the same port number for MIDI out and MIDI in.

Plan B for any DAW: run the standalone app, choose your microphone, and pick a MIDI output. On Mac, turn on the built-in IAC Driver. On Windows, install the free loopMIDI and select its port. Then enable that port as a MIDI input in your DAW.

Where it stands

VoxMidi is a new project. The engine is tested against synthetic voices and drums at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, and the plugins build automatically for Windows and Mac. Real voices are messier than test signals, so expect to adjust stickiness, pitch confidence and the gate until it feels right for you.

Found something that does not work? Open an issue and tell us your DAW, operating system and microphone.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. VoxMidi is free software under the GNU AGPL v3. You can use it, share it, and change it. If you share a changed version, you share its source code too.

Do I need a special microphone?

No. Any microphone works. A dynamic microphone, like the common stage vocal mics, picks up less room noise, which helps note tracking.

Does it work outside FL Studio?

Yes. It is a VST3 plugin, and on Mac also an AU. Any host that passes MIDI out of a plugin can use it. For every other case, use the standalone app with a virtual MIDI port.

Is this Dubler?

No. VoxMidi is an independent project built around the same idea of playing MIDI with your voice. It shares no code with Dubler and is not affiliated with Vochlea.

How fast is it?

In our tests a sung note starts about 30 ms after you begin, and a trained drum sound fires about 7 ms after the hit. Lowering stickiness makes notes start sooner; raising it ignores more wobble.
